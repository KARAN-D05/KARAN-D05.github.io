I build systems from first principles to understand how structure gives rise to computation, and how computation gives rise to intelligence.

My work moves through arithmetic machinery, stored-program architectures, hardware-level learning models, and the physical consequences of digital design.

This site is a record of that exploration.

Harvard Processor

A parameterized multi-cycle Harvard architecture processor, built around a custom instruction set and a hardwired control system. The work extends from RTL and self-checking verification through Sky130 synthesis, timing analysis, and post-synthesis evaluation.

02

High-Performance Adders

Six 64-bit adder architectures explored as different answers to the same question: how should carry information move through a machine? The study continues from RTL into synthesis, timing, PPA comparison, GLS, and selected physical implementation.

03

High-Performance Multipliers

A study of multiplication as an arithmetic datapath. Shift-and-add, Braun array, Wallace tree, and Dadda tree architectures are characterized through synthesis and timing, with the final carry-propagation stage connected back to the adder work.

04

Gate-Level Cybernetic Classifier

An experiment in whether adaptive, learning-like behavior can emerge from deterministic digital structure. The system evolves from strict Boolean pattern relations to popcount-based decisions and finally feedback-driven threshold adaptation.
