Building computation from first principles.

I design digital systems from the transistor up: arithmetic circuits, stored-program machines, and hardware that adapts, to understand how structure gives rise to computation, and how computation gives rise to intelligence.

"Dovere is my hand, tho small,
May I not add with truth;
I do my best to please you all,
Encourage then my youth." The Maillardet Automaton, c. 1800

My work follows one line: from arithmetic circuits and stored-program machines, through a full multi-cycle processor, to hardware that adapts and learns with no software layer above it. Every project is carried through to silicon-level detail: synthesized, timed, and measured on real standard-cell libraries rather than stopped at a functional simulation.

I'm drawn to the boundary between structure and intelligence: how a small set of logic gates, wired correctly, starts to behave like memory, judgment, or learning.
